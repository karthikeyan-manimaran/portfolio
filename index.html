02 Capstone · BCSE497J

A quadrotor that tilts its thrust to take off and land on slopes.

Adaptive Takeoff & Landing Multirotor UAV for Uneven Surfaces — every motor on its own 2-DOF pan-tilt mount, with a ROS 2 controller that vectors thrust while PX4 keeps ownership of motor speed.

01 · Problem

A multirotor takes off and lands reliably only on flat ground. On a slope the thrust of every fixed motor leans with the frame and the vehicle slides or tips. Articulated landing gear does nothing in flight; tilting rotors aren't normally used to level thrust on the ground.

02 · Approach

  • Four independent 2-DOF pan-tilt mounts, 3D-printed; bench-verified safe range 50°–130°, 40° combined clamp.
  • ROS 2 hybrid tilt controller: IMU-direct and Pixhawk-driven sources, 0.30 s stale fallback.
  • Watchdog, altitude lockout, flight-phase state machine, staged bring-up.

03 · Results & failures

  • Latency fixed: a MAVLink stream cap, a moving average and console logging → 90 → 6.7 ms.
  • Yaw drift isolated to PX4 and the tilt loop correcting the same axes.
  • A takeoff crash traced to spin-up vibration → angle & gyro deadbands.
  • Status: bench + indoor hover working; final controller flown, measured results being compiled.
